01

Visual direction

Trauma Care sits between three worlds: a registered charity, a professional membership body, and a clinical education provider. The design has to earn trust from a consultant surgeon and a first-year student paramedic on the same page.

The organising idea: the chain of care

The logo shows three figures whose linked arms form chain links. That metaphor already runs through the charity's language — "best practice throughout the whole chain of care", and "from the moment of injury, to the return to active life". It becomes the visual system: connected shapes, continuous rules, cards that link into grids, and a single unbroken green thread running through every page.

Four principles

Clinical, not corporate

Generous whitespace, a restrained palette and a clear hierarchy. Information is scannable under time pressure. Nothing decorative competes with content.

Human at the centre

Real photography of practitioners, real names, real outcomes. The red figure at the heart of the logo is the patient — people lead, systems follow.

Accessible by construction

AA contrast is designed in, not retrofitted. Keyboard operation, visible focus and semantic structure are properties of the components, so no page can regress.

One system, two audiences

The public site and the member area share every token and component. The member area is the same design at a higher density, not a separate product.

What changes from the current site

Today Six typefaces on one page, headings that get smaller as they go up the hierarchy, brand green used for small text at 2.68:1, dropdown menus that cannot be reached by keyboard, and no clean URLs.
Proposed Two typefaces, one descending type scale, a green ramp with defined text-safe steps, navigation that works without JavaScript, and a component set built for editors to reuse.

Accessibility

WCAG 2.1 AA is a contractual requirement covering the public site and the member area. These decisions are built into the components so pages inherit them rather than having to remember them.

Focus — tab through these to see the ring on every ground
Green button Neutral button An inline link
The same ring on the dark ground
Inverse button Outline button

A yellow inner ring with a dark outer ring stays visible on white, on brand green, on photography and on navy — one treatment for the whole site, rather than the browser default that disappears against green.

Accessibility commitments and how each is met
CriterionHow the system meets it
1.4.3 ContrastEvery text token is measured and published above. Green 500 is barred from text use.
1.4.1 Use of colourErrors, gating and status always carry an icon and words, never colour alone.
1.4.4 Resize textFluid clamp() sizing in rem; layouts reflow to 200% zoom without loss.
2.1.1 KeyboardNavigation is real links and buttons. Nothing depends on hover or on JavaScript to be reachable.
2.4.1 Bypass blocksA skip link is the first focusable element on every page.
2.4.7 Focus visibleThe two-tone ring above, applied through :focus-visible.
2.5.8 Target sizeInteractive controls are at least 44 × 44 CSS pixels.
2.3.3 Animationprefers-reduced-motion disables transitions and smooth scrolling.
3.3.1 Error identificationAn error summary at the top of the form links to each failed field.
3.3.2 LabelsVisible labels on every control. Placeholders are never used as labels.
1.3.1 Info & relationshipsLandmarks, one h1 per page, a descending heading order, and fieldset/legend on grouped controls.
Forced coloursComponents keep their shape in Windows High Contrast mode.
